# Trading bot audit — 11 October 2026

This audit covers the trigger and fair/Kelly decision path, live HTTP/2 sender, legacy SDK execution, paper broker, Position Manager, public/private feeds, order books, REST recovery, accounting, resolution, startup/shutdown, and the test runner. Production strategy defaults, trigger math, fair-value math, Kelly sizing, price limits, cooldowns, exposure limits, certificate verification, and kill switches are preserved. Safety checks now reject several previously accepted invalid or stale states.

The measurements below distinguish local processing from exchange/network delay. Only loopback fake venues received test orders. Public network probes used unauthenticated reads and market-data subscriptions; no funded order was submitted.

## Corrections

- The fast HTTP/2 path releases timed-out/cancelled streams; resolves pending requests on resets, GOAWAY, write failures and malformed responses; refuses foreign origins; enforces peer limits before writing; and closes failed connections. An uncertain write is recorded once and cannot fall through to a second POST. Certificate trust-store loading happens once off the event loop.
- Prepared orders recheck market identity, book freshness, private feed coverage, collateral freshness, expiry, clock trust, kill switches and canary budgets immediately before writing, including after a cold connection. Changed signing/sizing rules invalidate an older prepared payload. Balance reads cannot refund collateral reserved or spent by a concurrent order. Cancellation keeps ambiguous exposure and halts new orders.
- Market and private subscriptions wake immediately on discovery changes instead of waiting for 250/500 ms polling. Private application heartbeat starts on connection, with readiness withheld until a PONG. Reconnection clears subscription/socket state even if the initial send fails. Heartbeats keep a fixed cadence and failed warm probes use bounded jittered recovery.
- Invalid or missing exchange timestamps and malformed book updates fail closed. Modern WebSocket receive queues are measured correctly. REST snapshots cannot overwrite newer WebSocket changes or sync a later session with pre-disconnection data. Independent token resyncs run concurrently with a bounded limit. Binance connection failures advance to its configured fallback endpoint.
- Paper order slot locks are claimed before registry pruning; books must match the requested token; market identity and expiry are rechecked at booking. SDK client initialization and ambiguity registries are synchronized. Installing the paper-mode firewall waits for an exit already signing/posting before returning. Invalid uint256 token IDs and overflowing collateral, stakes or round timestamps fail closed before client access. The legacy retry after an explicit no-match no longer waits a fixed 500 ms; it still revalidates the same amount and limit. Timeout/error text alone cannot authorize a retry. Ambiguous orders are never retried.
- Position Manager workers use a dedicated two-thread pool and retain completion callbacks independently of an asyncio waiter's cancellation. Unknown outcomes keep reservations; late committed fills book once; failed dispatch releases an unsent reservation. Shutdown blocks new dispatch and drains completion before closing journals and final ledger persistence.
- Windows state-file replacement retries only transient sharing/access conflicts for a bounded 35 ms total backoff; journal durability and persistent-error halts are retained. Concurrent canary saves are serialized so an older periodic write cannot overwrite final shutdown counters. No venue order submission is retried by the filesystem helper.
- On-chain resolution requires proved token-to-payout-slot mapping or the existing independent CLOB/Gamma mirror agreement before accepting an unproven mapping. The direct fetch path no longer assumes CLOB row order. Invalid payouts fail closed. Rejected sales cannot create/reopen a position or mutate FIFO inventory; numerical overflow is rejected before mutation. Persisted FIFO/sale fees are validated, cash reconciliation includes net sale proceeds, failed settlement-journal flushes retain events, and discovery rejects a response that arrived after its requested round expired.

## Final local latency

Measured at 23:13:50 local time on 11 October 2026, Windows 11 / Python 3.13.13. Command:

```text
python bench_execution.py --fair --pm stress --orders 1000 --updates 10000 --sign 300 --journal 300 --wakeup 200 --json data/audit_latency_final.json
```

The actual run submitted **1,100/1,100 fixture orders**, including 100 overlapping UP/DOWN pairs, through one warm HTTP/2 connection. There were no hot-path connection creations, gate skips, halts, persistence failures or matched-but-unresolved orders. Fair/Kelly evaluation and repeated Position Manager evaluation ran alongside the sender. The fake venue produced 220 confirmed fills and definitive no-fill responses for the other orders.

Coinbase also had eight ticker gaps above two seconds, with a maximum 5.936 s gap, while heartbeats continued. A healthy connection does not guarantee a fresh price; the execution freshness gates remain necessary. Raw public results are in `data/audit_network.json`.

The machine's resolver returned the same address, `195.175.254.2`, for the CLOB, Gamma and Polymarket WebSocket hosts. Together with the timeouts and certificate failure, this suggests a network/resolver or interception problem; the precise cause was not established. A usable route and valid TLS are required before live validation can succeed. Certificate verification remains enabled.

[Polymarket's changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog) states that crypto taker matching has a 150 ms delay since 4 September 2026. For an order subject to that delay, under 50 ms from trigger to actual matching is technically impossible even with negligible local processing. Warm local trigger-to-write can meet the target; real acknowledgement, matching and confirmation depend on venue policy, network placement and exchange load.

## Evidence and limits

The new regressions exercise real loopback WebSocket/HTTP/2 servers plus deterministic fault injection. Existing tests cover strategy replay, independent fair/Kelly formula checks, fees, partial/no fills, authorization, duplicate fills, exposure/cooldown, settlement, restart recovery and dashboard behavior. The test environment blocks external DNS/connections and excludes production credentials.

Benchmarks use real signing with a disposable key, a warm loopback HTTP/2 fake venue and synthetic data. Fair/Kelly gates are enabled in the final benchmark, but benchmark cooldowns and capacity limits are lifted solely to generate repeatable load. Simulated acknowledgements and fills are not exchange executions. Sustained CPU contention can still cause event-loop delays above 50 ms; the benchmark measures both ordinary dashboard cadence and an intentionally continuous-render stress case.

This is a source audit and a bounded test campaign, not a guarantee of absence of bugs or permanent connection uptime. Real Polymarket order acknowledgement/fill speed and long-running connection stability remain unverified on this host. No live canary or deployment was started.

The inactive legacy live-exit helper still has no durable sell reconciliation/authorization workflow. It now rechecks eligibility after signing and blocks its token after an uncertain or accepted-pending result, preventing repeated submissions. That process-local block has no automatic unblock; an operator must reconcile the outcome before deliberately restarting/reusing that helper. The current canary continues to use its existing BUY strategy.
